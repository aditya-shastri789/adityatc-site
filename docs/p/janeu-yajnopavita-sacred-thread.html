Siddhak Janeu for puja (also called Yajnopavita, Yagyopavit, Janeyu or sacred thread) is one of the most revered symbols in Sanatana Dharma. Worn across the left shoulder and resting on the right side, the Janeu represents purity, discipline, responsibility and a life dedicated to Dharma.

It is first worn at the Upanayana (sacred thread ceremony) and is then worn during daily Sandhya Vandana, Gayatri japa, homas, yajnas and all major Hindu rituals. Our Janeu is made with high-quality cotton threads, twisted and knotted in the traditional way.

Meaning and tradition

  • Symbol of the "twice-born" (Dvija): the Upanayanam marks a second birth into spiritual learning, Vedic study and righteous conduct.
  • The three strands are interpreted as body, mind and speech; sattva, rajas and tamas (the three gunas); or the duties owed to Devas, Rishis and Pitris.
  • Worn daily, it is a reminder to live a disciplined life, follow one's dharma, chant the Gayatri mantra and stay connected to the Divine.

Benefits

  • Traditional design and proportions: made following customary Vedic guidelines for length, knotting and number of strands
  • High-quality cotton thread: soft on the skin, strong enough for everyday use
  • Sacred knot (Brahma Granthi) tied in the traditional manner
  • Lightweight and comfortable for daily wear during work, study, prayer and meditation
  • Ritual-ready and clean for homas, yajnas, vratas, festivals and temple visits

Ideal for

  • Upanayana / sacred thread ceremony (Yajnopavita samskara)
  • Daily Sandhya Vandana, Gayatri mantra japa and nitya karma
  • Priests (purohits), devotees, temples and household havan, yagya, homa and vratas
  • Griha Pravesh, Vivah (wedding rituals), Pitru pooja, Satyanarayan pooja, Navratri, Diwali Lakshmi pooja
  • Wearing or renewing on Upakarma, Raksha Bandhan, Janmashtami, Navaratri, Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali

How to use

  • Traditionally worn over the left shoulder and across the chest, resting on the right side.
  • Usually worn after snana (bath) and sankalpa, as per family or Guru's tradition.
  • Many families change the Janeu every year on Upakarma or during specific festivals.
  • Change it if it becomes impure, damaged or worn out, as per traditional guidelines.

Care

  • Hand wash gently in clean water when required; avoid harsh detergents and vigorous wringing.
  • Keep in a clean, dry place away from dust when not in use.
