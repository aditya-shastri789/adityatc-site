Siddhak Kalawa / Moli thread for pooja (also called Mauli, Kalava, Mouli, Raksha Sutra or Pooja Mauli) is a sacred red and yellow cotton thread used in Hindu rituals, ceremonies and daily worship. Traditionally tied on the wrist during pooja, homa and festivals, Kalawa is believed to invoke divine blessings, protection and good fortune.

The 10 metre roll gives ample length for family rituals, temple use, festivals, home pooja and spiritual gatherings.

Meaning and tradition

  • Raksha Sutra: often tied on the wrist by a priest or elder while chanting mantras, as a sacred bond of protection and blessings.
  • Red signifies strength, energy, Shakti and auspiciousness; yellow represents purity, wisdom and the grace of Lord Vishnu and Goddess Lakshmi.
  • It is usually tied after taking a sankalpa, as a reminder to stay committed to one's prayers and vows.

Benefits

  • 10 metre continuous roll for many poojas, functions, temple rituals and festivals
  • Traditional red-yellow colour combination
  • High-quality cotton: soft on the skin, strong for wrist-tying and decoration, easy to cut and tie
  • Rolled format: cut only the length you need and keep the rest neat and tangle-free

Common uses

  • Wrist-tying (Raksha Sutra): in many traditions on the right wrist for men and unmarried boys, and on the left wrist for women and girls
  • Ganesh pooja, Satyanarayan Katha, Navratri and Durga pooja, Lakshmi pooja and Diwali, Hanuman pooja, Shiv pooja, homas and havans
  • Tying around the kalash at griha pravesh, weddings and major ceremonies
  • Wrapping coconuts, the pooja thali and deity photos or idols
  • Griha Pravesh, weddings and engagements, thread ceremonies, naamkaran and other samskaras
  • Navratri, Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, temple rituals and satsangs
  • Offering in temples and tying to sacred trees, railings or mandir doors
  • Daily pooja and yajnas by priests (purohits) and pandits
  • New beginnings: new home, new vehicle, shop opening, exams, business start

How to use

  • During pooja: take a small length, hold it during sankalpa, and the priest or elder ties it on the wrist while chanting mantras.
  • For kalash and pooja setup: cut the length you need and wrap it around the kalash, coconut or pooja chowki, or use it to decorate the thali and bind small sacred items.
  • For daily and festival use: keep the roll in your home mandir and tie fresh Kalawa on important days, vratas and new undertakings.

Care and storage

  • Store in a clean, dry place, preferably with your pooja items, away from moisture and dust.
  • Use clean scissors to cut the required length.
